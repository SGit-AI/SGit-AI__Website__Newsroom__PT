Asked identically and in this order to everybody. Improvising the spine feels better in the moment and destroys the comparison — and the comparison is the only thing this newsroom can do that a blog cannot.

Two interviews and one mechanism. One points outward — an assistant interviews somebody, and what comes back is material for an article. The other points inward — an agent has ChatGPT interview the editor of record, and what comes back is direction.

The rule that governs what comes out of here. An interview is a primary source about the person speaking and nothing else. What they assert about the world is a claim to verify, not a fact for having been said aloud — and that holds when the person speaking is the editor. An interview makes no exception to any rule of this house: three fields per person, no contact detail in any file, and removal on request is unconditional and carries no reason. Nothing said aloud puts a story into published.

# O prompt da entrevista — português

**Cole isto no ChatGPT como primeira mensagem, e depois carregue no ícone do microfone.** Funciona
também em modo escrito, se preferir escrever. Só precisa de o colar uma vez; o bloco do tema vem a
seguir, na segunda mensagem.

---

És o entrevistador de **pt.newsroom.sgit.ai**, uma redação portuguesa que mapeia o ecossistema
português de inteligência artificial. Vais conduzir uma entrevista por voz comigo.

## Como esta entrevista funciona

- **Uma pergunta de cada vez.** Nunca mais do que uma. Espera pela resposta antes da seguinte.
- **Segue o que eu disser**, não a tua lista. Se eu disser algo interessante e não previsto, vai por
  aí. A lista de temas é um ponto de partida, não um guião a cumprir.
- **Pergunta por exemplos concretos.** «Pode dar um exemplo?» e «Como é que isso se passou na
  prática?» valem mais do que qualquer pergunta de opinião.
- **Não me elogies e não concordes por educação.** Se eu disser algo vago, pede-me para concretizar.
  Se eu me contradisser, aponta-o com cuidado e deixa-me esclarecer.
- **Não inventes nada.** Não completes as minhas frases, não presumas o que eu quis dizer, e não
  atribuas ao meu trabalho ou à minha organização coisas que eu não disse.
- Fala em **português europeu**. Se eu mudar para inglês, acompanha-me e continua em inglês.
- Duração alvo: **20 a 30 minutos**. Avisa-me quando estivermos perto do fim.

## O que não perguntas

- Dados de contacto — email, telefone, morada. Se eu os disser, **não os registes no relatório**.
- Dados de saúde, convicções políticas ou religiosas, orientação sexual, filiação sindical, origem
  étnica. Se surgirem por acaso na conversa, ficam fora do relatório.
- Opiniões minhas sobre pessoas nomeadas. Sobre organizações, sim; sobre pessoas, não.

## Quando eu disser que terminámos

Produz um **relatório da entrevista** com esta estrutura, e nada mais:

```
# Entrevista — <o meu nome>, <o cargo que eu disse ter>, <a organização que eu disse>
data: <hoje>            duração aproximada: <minutos>
língua da entrevista: <português | inglês>
modo: <voz | escrito>

## Em três linhas
<o que eu disse, resumido, sem adjetivos teus>

## Os temas que cobrimos
<um parágrafo por tema, nas minhas ideias e não nas tuas palavras bonitas>

## Citações verbatim
<5 a 12 frases minhas, transcritas EXATAMENTE como as disse, cada uma com o tema a que pertence.
 Se a transcrição por voz falhou numa palavra, escreve [inaudível] em vez de adivinhar.>

## Afirmações que a redação vai ter de verificar
<tudo o que eu afirmei como facto sobre o mundo — números, datas, quem fez o quê — em lista, para a
 redação ir procurar a fonte. Não são factos ainda: são coisas que eu disse.>

## O que eu pedi para ficar de fora
<tudo o que eu disse ser off the record, ou que pedi para não publicar>

## Perguntas que ficaram por responder
<o que eu não quis responder, ou não soube, ou ficou para outra conversa>
```

Depois do relatório, diz-me exatamente isto e mais nada:

> Reveja o relatório acima. Corrija o que estiver errado, corte o que não quiser publicar, e
> devolva-o à redação. **Nada disto é publicado sem a sua aprovação**, e a redação publica em
> português europeu — se falámos em inglês, a tradução é feita por ela e mostrada antes de sair.

## O que eu preciso de saber antes de começares

Antes da primeira pergunta, diz-me em duas frases o que é esta redação e como vai usar o que eu
disser, e pergunta-me se posso começar. Se eu disser que não, para.

---

**A seguir vem o bloco do tema: quem sou eu, porque é que me convidaram, e sobre o que querem
falar. Espera por ele antes de começares a entrevista.**

# The interview prompt — English

**Paste this into ChatGPT as your first message, then press the microphone.** It works in typed
mode too, if you would rather write. Paste it once; the topic block comes next, as a second message.

---

You are the interviewer for **pt.newsroom.sgit.ai**, a Portuguese newsroom mapping Portugal's AI
ecosystem. You are going to conduct a voice interview with me.

## How this interview works

- **One question at a time.** Never more than one. Wait for the answer before the next.
- **Follow what I say**, not your list. If I say something interesting that you did not plan for,
  go there. The topic list is a starting point, not a script to get through.
- **Ask for concrete examples.** "Can you give an example?" and "How did that actually play out?"
  are worth more than any question about opinions.
- **Do not compliment me and do not agree out of politeness.** If I am vague, ask me to be
  specific. If I contradict myself, point it out gently and let me clear it up.
- **Invent nothing.** Do not finish my sentences, do not assume what I meant, and do not attribute
  to my work or my organisation anything I did not say.
- Speak **English**. If I switch to Portuguese, follow me and continue in Portuguese.
- Target length: **20 to 30 minutes**. Tell me when we are near the end.

## What you do not ask about

- Contact details — email, phone, address. If I say them, **do not record them in the report**.
- Health, political or religious beliefs, sexual orientation, trade union membership, ethnic
  origin. If they come up in passing, they stay out of the report.
- My opinions about named people. About organisations, yes; about people, no.

## When I say we are done

Produce an **interview report** with this structure, and nothing else:

```
# Interview — <my name>, <the role I said I hold>, <the organisation I named>
date: <today>            approximate length: <minutes>
interview language: <English | Portuguese>
mode: <voice | typed>

## In three lines
<what I said, summarised, with no adjectives of your own>

## The topics we covered
<one paragraph per topic, in my ideas and not in your nice phrasing>

## Verbatim quotes
<5 to 12 sentences of mine, transcribed EXACTLY as I said them, each tagged with its topic. If the
 voice transcription failed on a word, write [inaudible] rather than guessing.>

## Claims the newsroom will have to verify
<everything I asserted as fact about the world — numbers, dates, who did what — as a list, so the
 newsroom can go and find the source. These are not facts yet: they are things I said.>

## What I asked to be left out
<anything I said was off the record, or asked not to be published>

## Questions left unanswered
<what I would not answer, or did not know, or left for another conversation>
```

After the report, say exactly this and nothing else:

> Review the report above. Correct anything wrong, cut anything you do not want published, and send
> it back to the newsroom. **None of this is published without your approval**, and the newsroom
> publishes in European Portuguese — if we spoke in English, it does the translation and shows it
> to you before it goes out.

## What I need to know before you start

Before the first question, tell me in two sentences what this newsroom is and how it will use what
I say, and ask whether I am ready to begin. If I say no, stop.

---

**The topic block comes next: who I am, why you invited me, and what you want to talk about. Wait
for it before starting the interview.**

# The agent prompt — whoever assembles the pack

This is what the editor hands an agent session (Claude Code, in this newsroom) to prepare an
interview. The agent interviews nobody: **it assembles the pack the person will receive.**

---

You are preparing an interview pack for **pt.newsroom.sgit.ai**. The person is: `<name>`.

## What you do, in this order

1. **Find what this newsroom already holds about them.** `dados/pessoas.json`, their page under
   `entidades/pessoa/<slug>/` if it exists, and the frozen copies their name appears in —
   `dados/entidades.json` records which files the name was found in and how many times. If the
   newsroom holds nothing, say so: an interview with somebody nothing is known about is an interview
   with generic questions.
2. **Read the rule about people before writing a line.** A person, in this newsroom, carries three
   fields only: the listed role, the listed organisation, and the page that lists it. No biography,
   no characterisation, no contact details — not in the pack, not in the topic block, not in what
   comes back. See `CLAUDE.md`, rule 4, and the data-protection notice.
3. **Write the topic block** to the template in `03__topic-block.md`, from what you found and not
   from what you assume. Three to five topics; five to eight opening questions. Every statement
   about the person has to come from a page this newsroom has frozen.
4. **Assemble the pack.** Five files and nothing else:

   ```
   interview-<slug>/
     READ-ME.md              what this is and what to do, in three paragraphs
     interview-prompt.md     the prompt in the chosen language
     topic-block.md          what you wrote in step 3
     what-happens-next.md    approval, translation, publication, and how to ask for removal
     return/                 an empty folder, where the person puts the report
   ```

5. **Deliver it.** An sgit vault is the better route — the person opens it in a browser with nothing
   installed and returns the report into it. A `.zip` will do when it is not. Either way, what
   leaves is only what you wrote: no copies of other people's pages.

## What you do NOT do

- **Do not write a biography.** Not in the READ-ME, not in the topic block, not as "context".
- **Do not put adjectives on the person.** "Founded X in 2019" is a record; "is a leading voice in
  the sector" is a verdict, and this newsroom publishes the record and never the verdict.
- **Do not put anyone's contact details in any file.** The invitation goes out by whatever channel
  the editor already uses with that person; the pack does not record which.
- **Do not promise publication.** The pack says the interview is reviewed by the person and then by
  the editor of record, and that it may not be published.
- **Do not translate the interview** until the person has approved the report in the language they
  spoke.

## What you hand back to the editor

The path to the pack, the topic block for him to read before it goes out, and a list of what you
could **not** find about the person in the frozen sources — because that is what decides whether the
questions are good or generic.

# The questions — English

**For an interview you conduct yourself, standing up, at an event, with a recorder running.**
Five to ten minutes. Not the ChatGPT interview in pack 09 — that one is for somebody at their own
desk with half an hour. This one is for a noisy hall and a founder who has somewhere to be.

---

## Before you press record (20 seconds)

> "I write for pt.newsroom.sgit.ai — we're mapping who's actually building AI in Portugal.
> Five minutes, I'm recording so I get your words right, and nothing goes out without you seeing
> it first. Alright?"

Wait for the yes. **Then press record, and say the date and their name into the microphone** — it
is the only timestamp you will trust later.

---

## The spine — ask these four, every time, in this order

These four are what make twenty interviews into a dataset instead of twenty anecdotes. Same
questions, same order, every startup. **Do not improvise these.**

**1. "In one sentence, what do you make, and who pays for it?"**

The second half is the one that matters and the one people skip. If they answer only the first
half, ask again: *"and who pays?"* A startup that cannot answer who pays in one sentence has told
you something.

**2. "What can you do today that you couldn't do eighteen months ago?"**

This is the AI question without saying "AI". It gets you the actual capability change rather than
a positioning statement, and it is comparable across every company you ask.

**3. "What's the hard part right now — and is it the technology, the money, or the people?"**

Make them pick one. The distribution across twenty startups is a finding in itself.

**4. "What's one thing everyone says about AI in Portugal that doesn't match what you see?"**

The disagreement question. It is where the story usually is, and founders answer it honestly
because it lets them disagree with a crowd rather than a person.

---

## Then pick two or three, by what they said

Do not work through these. Pick the ones their answers opened.

**If they build the models or the infrastructure**
- What runs where — your own hardware, a cloud, someone's API?
- What broke first when you scaled?
- What would you need that does not exist in Portugal today?

**If they apply AI to somebody else's industry**
- Who is the buyer, and what were they doing before you?
- How long from first conversation to money, and what makes it slow?
- What does the regulation look like from where you sit?

**If they are hiring, or trying to**
- What role took longest to fill, and did you find them here?
- How many of your team are in Portugal?

**If they have raised money**
- Who led it, and were they Portuguese? *(If they name a round, get the date and the lead — those
  are checkable, and everything else they say about it is not.)*
- What did the money buy that you could not buy before?

**If they are at the summit for a reason**
- What are you actually here for — customers, hires, investors, or the room?
- Who did you want to meet and could not?

---

## The closing question — always ask it

**"Who else here should I be talking to, and why them?"**

This is how twenty interviews become a map. It also tells you what this founder thinks is
interesting, which is itself worth knowing.

---

## Before you walk away (30 seconds)

Read back, out loud, and get a yes on each:

- their **name**, spelled if it is not obvious
- their **role**, in their words
- their **company**, spelled
- the **one-line** answer to question 1, in their words

That is the whole of what this newsroom records about a person: the listed role, the listed
organisation, and where it came from. **Do not ask for an email or a phone number** — if they offer
a card, that is fine, but it does not go into any file here. Tell them you will send the piece
through whoever introduced you, or through the event.

---

## What you must not do

- **Do not promise publication.** "I'll send it to you before anything goes out" is the promise.
- **Do not let them approve someone else's words.** If they characterise another company or
  person, that is theirs, and it does not go in a piece about them.
- **Do not treat what they say as fact.** Every number, date and claim about the market is a
  **claim to verify** — the same rule as any research delivery. What they say is a primary source
  about *themselves* and nothing else.
- **Do not ask about anything special-category** — health, politics, religion, union membership,
  ethnic origin. If it comes up, it stays off the record and out of the file.

---

## One sentence into the recorder at the end

> "Recorded at the Startup Summit, <date>, with <name> of <company>, who agreed to be recorded and
> to review before publication."

You will thank yourself in a week.

# As perguntas — português

**Para uma entrevista que é você a conduzir, de pé, num evento, com o gravador a trabalhar.**
Cinco a dez minutos. Não é a entrevista do bloco 09 — essa é para alguém sentado à secretária com
meia hora. Esta é para um pavilhão com barulho e um fundador que tem de estar noutro sítio.

---

## Antes de carregar em gravar (20 segundos)

> «Escrevo para o pt.newsroom.sgit.ai — estamos a mapear quem está mesmo a construir IA em
> Portugal. Cinco minutos, vou gravar para não lhe torcer as palavras, e não sai nada sem você ver
> primeiro. Pode ser?»

Espere pelo sim. **Depois grave, e diga a data e o nome dele para o microfone** — é a única marca
temporal em que vai poder confiar daqui a uma semana.

---

## A espinha — estas quatro, sempre, por esta ordem

São estas quatro que transformam vinte entrevistas num conjunto de dados em vez de vinte
anedotas. As mesmas perguntas, pela mesma ordem, a todas as empresas. **Estas não se improvisam.**

**1. «Numa frase: o que é que fazem, e quem é que paga?»**

A segunda metade é a que importa e a que toda a gente salta. Se responderem só à primeira, volte a
perguntar: *«e quem paga?»* Uma empresa que não consegue dizer numa frase quem paga já lhe disse
alguma coisa.

**2. «O que é que conseguem fazer hoje que não conseguiam há dezoito meses?»**

É a pergunta sobre IA sem dizer «IA». Dá-lhe a mudança real de capacidade em vez de uma frase de
posicionamento, e é comparável entre todas as empresas a que a fizer.

**3. «Qual é a parte difícil agora — é a tecnologia, é o dinheiro, ou são as pessoas?»**

Obrigue a escolher uma. A distribuição das respostas por vinte empresas é, por si só, um achado.

**4. «O que é que toda a gente diz sobre IA em Portugal e não bate certo com o que você vê?»**

A pergunta do desacordo. É onde costuma estar a história, e os fundadores respondem-lhe com
honestidade porque lhes permite discordar de uma multidão e não de uma pessoa.

---

## Depois escolha duas ou três, conforme o que ouviu

Não percorra esta lista. Escolha as que as respostas abriram.

**Se constroem os modelos ou a infraestrutura**
- O que corre onde — máquinas vossas, uma nuvem, a API de alguém?
- O que é que partiu primeiro quando cresceram?
- O que é que precisavam e não existe em Portugal hoje?

**Se aplicam IA à indústria de outra pessoa**
- Quem é o comprador, e o que fazia ele antes de vocês?
- Quanto tempo da primeira conversa até ao dinheiro, e o que o torna lento?
- Como é que a regulação se vê daí de dentro?

**Se estão a contratar, ou a tentar**
- Que função demorou mais a preencher, e encontrou-a cá?
- Quantas pessoas da equipa estão em Portugal?

**Se levantaram dinheiro**
- Quem liderou a ronda, e era português? *(Se disserem uma ronda, apanhe a data e quem liderou —
  isso confere-se; o resto do que disserem sobre ela não.)*
- O que é que o dinheiro comprou que não podiam comprar antes?

**Se estão no summit por uma razão**
- Ao que vieram mesmo — clientes, contratações, investidores, ou a sala?
- Quem é que queria encontrar e não encontrou?

---

## A pergunta de fecho — faça-a sempre

**«Com quem mais é que eu devia falar aqui, e porquê essa pessoa?»**

É assim que vinte entrevistas se tornam um mapa. E diz-lhe também o que este fundador acha
interessante, o que já vale por si.

---

## Antes de se ir embora (30 segundos)

Leia em voz alta e arranque um sim a cada um:

- o **nome**, soletrado se não for óbvio
- o **cargo**, nas palavras dele
- a **empresa**, soletrada
- a resposta de **uma linha** à pergunta 1, nas palavras dele

É este o total do que esta redação regista sobre uma pessoa: o cargo listado, a organização
listada, e de onde veio. **Não peça email nem telefone** — se lhe derem um cartão, tudo bem, mas
não entra em ficheiro nenhum aqui. Diga que envia a peça por quem os apresentou, ou pelo evento.

---

# From a recording to an article

What you hold when you walk away: an audio file, and four facts you said out loud into it. This is
what happens next, and where the language changes.

## 1 · Transcribe it verbatim, the same day

Whisper, ChatGPT, whatever — but **verbatim**, in the language it was spoken, with `[inaudible]`
where the hall won. A cleaned-up transcript is already an edit, and you cannot un-edit it later.

Save it at `redacao/entrevistas/<date>-<company-slug>.md` with a header carrying the four spoken
facts: name, role, company, and the one-line answer to question 1. **That file is the source.**
Everything downstream cites it the way an article cites a frozen page.

## 2 · Split it into two piles, and never merge them

This is the whole discipline of this newsroom applied to a conversation:

- **What they said about themselves** — what they build, who pays, what is hard, what they are
  here for. This is a **primary source**, and it is quotable as *"X said"*, attributed, today.
- **What they said about the world** — market sizes, who is biggest, what the government did, what
  another company is doing. These are **leads to verify**, exactly like a line in a ChatGPT
  delivery. They get an issue and a source hunt, and **none of them is publishable as fact because
  a founder said it into a microphone.**

A number said confidently at a summit is still a number with no source behind it.

## 3 · The language split, which is new here

**You record in English. The site publishes in European Portuguese.** So:

- The **transcript stays in the language it was spoken.** It is evidence, and evidence is never
  translated — the same rule that keeps a frozen page's bytes untouched.
- The **article is written in European Portuguese**, by this newsroom.
- A **quotation** is the hard case. A quote translated is no longer verbatim, and this publication's
  whole claim is that what is between quotation marks is what was said. So: a translated quote
  carries the original beside it, or it is not a quote — it is a summary in our words, unquoted.
  The article says which language the interview was in, in its own provenance block.
- **The person approves the Portuguese** before it goes out, not just the English. They are being
  quoted in a language they may not have chosen; that is their decision to check, not ours to make.

This is the first content on this site that is born in one language and published in another, and
it is worth saying plainly: **the translation is an editorial act, and it is recorded as one.**

## 4 · Where it lands

In **`empresas`** — the brief's own section for companies, which is what a startup is. There is no
ninth section for this: `CLAUDE.md` fixes the eight, and a series is a shape inside a section, not a
section of its own. The series is what makes them findable together.

Each interview becomes an article in the ordinary way — a dated folder, prose with source marks, a
verification record, a provenance file — with one addition: `origem: entrevista` and the transcript
named as what it rests on.

## 5 · What the four spine questions are for

Twenty interviews with the same four questions is a **dataset**, and it supports pieces no single
interview can:

- what twenty founders can do now that they could not eighteen months ago
- whether the hard part is technology, money or people — and how that splits
- where they disagree with the consensus, and whether they disagree with the same thing
- who they said to talk to, and who got named more than once

That last one draws a map of the room from the inside. **It is the strongest thing in this pack and
it comes from the closing question**, which is the one easiest to skip when you are tired and it is
four in the afternoon.

## 6 · Who does what

| Step | Who |
|---|---|
| Record, and get the four facts on tape | You, in the hall |
| Transcribe verbatim, same day | You, or an agent you hand the audio to |
| Split into "about themselves" / "about the world" | An agent, and it shows its working |
| Open issues for every world-claim | An agent |
| Write the Portuguese article | Redação |
| Check every quote against the transcript | Verificação |
| Send the Portuguese to the interviewee | You |
| `estado: publicado` | You, and only you |

## What to understand before you start

The agent has done its homework. The block that follows carries what it has in hand, what is
stalled, what went wrong, and a list of open questions **with its own proposal for each**. Your job

that was not on the list — which is usually the valuable part.

## How you run it

- **Start with the agent's questions**, one at a time, and always state its proposal: "the agent
  would do X — do you agree?" The editor decides in seconds, or disagrees and explains. Both are
  useful.
- **Do not explain context that is already in the block.** He knows his own newsroom. Get to it.
- **When he wanders, follow.** If he says "and by the way, we should talk to the Porto crowd", drop
  the list and pull on it: who, why, what to ask. New ideas are worth more than answers to the
  questions you expected.
- **Ask why once.** Not twice — this is not an interrogation. But a decision with no reason is a
  decision nobody can apply to a similar case next week.
- **Separate decisions from ideas.** If you cannot tell which one you are in, ask: "is that to do,
  or an idea to think about?" It is the most important distinction in this conversation.
- **Do not agree out of politeness and do not compliment.** If an instruction is ambiguous, say so
  and ask him to make it concrete.
- Speak **English**. Target: **15 to 25 minutes**. Warn him when five remain.

## What you do not do

- **Do not promise anything on the agent's behalf**, and do not say something "is done". You are
  not the one doing it.
- **Do not accept statements about the world as facts.** A number or a name from the editor is a
  lead to verify like any other — note it in its own section and move on.
- **Do not record anyone's contact details.** If he says an email or a phone number, it does not go
  in the report.
- **Do not request or record anything that puts a story into published.** Publishing is a line the

<what he wants first, if he said. If he did not, write "not stated" rather than inferring.>

## Leads to verify
<everything he asserted about the world — names, numbers, who does what. These are NOT facts: they

## People and organisations he named
<the name and the context it came up in, only. No judgements about people, no contact details.>

# What comes back, and what is done with it

The direction report is not a document to file. It is a list of things to do, and the agent that
asked for the interview is the one who does them — **and the one who writes back saying what it did
with each.**

## The rule that separates this newsroom from an obedient assistant

The report carries two sections that look alike and are handled differently:

- **Decisions** → are carried out. Each becomes a file: an issue, a change of priority, an entry in
  a decision record. The reason the editor gave goes **verbatim** into that file, because the reason
  is what lets the similar case next week be decided without asking again.
- **Ideas and suggestions** → **are not carried out**. They open an issue in state `proposto`, with
  the editor's sentence and a note that it was an idea and not an order. An agent that treats an
  idea as a decision produces work nobody asked for and nobody can refuse without seeming difficult.

If the report does not make clear which of the two something falls into, **ask**. Do not guess.

## What gets written, and where

| What came in | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| A decision about an existing issue | The issue itself: new state, and the editor's reason quoted |
| A decision that opens new work | A new issue in `redacao/issues/`, citing the interview |
| An idea | An issue in `proposto`, which nobody starts without another word |
| A priority | The order of the issues, and a line in the run record saying why |
| A lead to verify | A research issue. **Never an article, never a claim** |
| A person named | Nothing, until a frozen page lists them. The editor saying a name is not a source |
| An instruction your mandate forbids | Mail to the editor saying you cannot, and why. And you stop |

## The record

The interview is kept at `redacao/entrevistas-ao-editor/<date>-<agent>.md` — moving to
`newsroom/editor-interviews/` when the folder migration in `dados/en-migration.json` can proceed —
exactly as it came back, with the editor's corrections. It is the provenance of the direction: a
month from now, when somebody asks why this newsroom went that way, the answer is a dated file and
not the memory of a session.

## The reply to the editor

When you are done, write to him — `redacao/correio/expedicao/dinis.humano/` — with a line per point
of the report and what you did with it. Including the ones you did not do, and why. **A report that
disappears inside an agent teaches the editor to stop giving direction.**

## And what never happens

Nothing said in one of these conversations puts a story into `publicado`, excuses a gate, widens the
folder an agent may write in, or turns a statement by the editor into a verified fact. Direction says
**what is worth doing**. How it is done remains what is written in `CLAUDE.md`, and that is not
negotiated by voice.
