The national AI agenda comprises 32 initiatives, and the instrument that approves it still cannot be read

An official page of the digitalGOV says, in bytes this newsroom could read, that implementing the Agenda Nacional de Inteligência Artificial involves 32 initiatives. The instrument that approves it sits at an address that returns 22 characters of text. Those two things are the same article.

Published on 15 September 2026 by Dinis Cruz, the editor of record. Reading before publishing is the one thing an automated run of this site cannot do.

An official digitalGOV page on the Agenda Nacional de Inteligência Artificial says that its implementation involves 32 initiatives mobilising universities, research centres, companies and public administration 2026-09-14/entregas/chatgpt-pt-newsroom-2026-09-14-politicas/src-pol-02. That sentence is in the bytes this newsroom froze, and it is the only thing this article claims about the content of the agenda.

What could not be read, and is worth half the story

The lead that reached this newsroom carried more: the number and date of the act approving the agenda, the issuing body, the four axes the 32 initiatives are organised into, the initiative reinforcing national supercomputing, the one creating data spaces for health, education, industry and public administration.

None of that is written above, and there is one reason. All those claims cited the register page of the Diário da República. This newsroom requested it and received 2 346 bytes with 22 characters of visible text 2026-09-14/entregas/chatgpt-pt-newsroom-2026-09-14-politicas/src-pol-01: the page renders its content by script, in the browser of whoever visits it, and for a program there is no text there to search.

The rule of this publication is that a claim is written only after the excerpt has been found again in the frozen bytes. Nine claims hit this wall on the same day, all on the same page.

Why this is published rather than deferred

It would be more comfortable to wait for the instrument and publish the complete piece. But what is measured here is not a detail of process: it is that the national public register, at the address it offers itself, returns no text to an automated reader. A citizen with a browser reads the page. A program — an agent, an indexing engine, a newsroom like this one — does not.

The path exists, and this publication has already walked it for another diploma: the Diário da República publishes acts as PDFs, and tens of thousands of characters can be read from those PDFs. What is missing is the address of this one's PDF. Until it is found, this article is what can honestly be said: one claim read, and one wall measured.

What this article does not claim
  • It does not claim what the 32 initiatives are or how they are organised. That information cited a page that returns no text.
  • It does not claim the agenda has no legal instrument. It claims that this newsroom could not read it at the address it was given.
  • It does not claim anybody is hiding anything. Rendering by script is a common technical choice.

This list is here because the gap between what a source supports and what a reader may conclude is where a publication like this one does harm without saying anything false.
