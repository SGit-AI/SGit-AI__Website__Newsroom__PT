The regulator was designated and the designating instrument is not on the Government's AI page

The Regulamento Europeu de IA requires every Member State to designate its national competent authorities by 2 August 2025. The Portuguese Government's topic page describes the regulation and names no authority. This newsroom did not find the designating act, and does not claim it does not exist.

Published on 15 September 2026 by Dinis Cruz, the editor of record. Reading before publishing is the one thing an automated run of this site cannot do.

The Regulamento Europeu de Inteligência Artificial assigns enforcement to the Member States, and the Comissão Europeia says on its governance and delivery page that every Member State should have designated and empowered its national competent authorities by 2 August 2025 2026-09-14/entregas/chatgpt-pt-newsroom-2026-09-14-politicas/src-pol-07.

The official Portuguese topic page on artificial intelligence describes the regulation — it says that establishes the first horizontal legal framework for the development, placing on the market and use of AI systems in the European Union 2026-09-14/entregas/chatgpt-pt-newsroom-2026-09-14-politicas/src-pol-06 — and, in the text this newsroom froze and read, does not name the national authority.

That absence is the article. It is not the absence of a designation: it is the absence of a name on the page a citizen finds first when they ask who regulates AI in Portugal.

What this newsroom tried and could not do

A research lead pointed this newsroom at an ANACOM page which, according to whoever found it, presents the body as nationally responsible for applying the regulation. On requesting that page, this newsroom received HTTP 403.

A 403 is a server refusing a client. It is not a refusal of information, it is not a secret, and it is not a fact about ANACOM — it is a fact about what robots.txt would win a paragraph and lose the argument that makes this publication different from an aggregator. So the claim that rested on that page was not written, and is recorded as unchecked.

Nor was it located, in the Diário da República, the act designating the market surveillance authority or the notifying authority. The search remains open.

The difference this article refuses to erase

There are two possible sentences here and only one of them is true from the bytes this newsroom holds:

  • «Portugal não designou as suas autoridades nacionais.» Isto seria uma afirmação sobre o mundo, e

this newsroom cannot make it: it has not read the whole register, and a designation may exist in an instrument it has not yet found.

  • «Esta redação procurou e não encontrou o ato de designação; e a página temática do Governo, na

cópia que congelou, não nomeia autoridade nenhuma.» Isto é uma afirmação sobre o registo público e sobre esta busca, e é verificável por quem quiser repeti-la.

The second is the one that is published. The first is still a question, and a dated question is worth more than an invented answer.

What this article does not claim
  • It does not claim that no authority was designated. It claims what is and is not on the pages this newsroom could read, with dates and hashes.
  • It does not claim that ANACOM is or is not the authority. The page that would say so returned 403 to this newsroom and nothing was checked against it.
  • It does not characterise the Government, ANACOM or any other named party. The record is published, never the verdict.

This list is here because the gap between what a source supports and what a reader may conclude is where a publication like this one does harm without saying anything false.
