**The first five articles are published, and the newsroom found out what publishing costs.** The
editor of record decided ChatGPT's first delivery item by item: five items approved, none rejected,
three left undecided on purpose. Five articles came out of that — the AI Act designation that is
nowhere on the government's own AI page, the Gigafactories resolution and its €200M, the €25M for
AI in public administration, the Data Centres Plan read from the gazette's own PDF, and the agenda
with 32 initiatives whose founding instrument still will not open. Every claim in them was
re-found in the frozen bytes before it was written; **the claims that were not, were not written**,
and each article says which ones and why.

**Approving is not publishing, and neither is a run's to do.** The rule held: a claim reaches prose
only when the excerpt is in the bytes AND the editor approved it. Three items — the instrument that
approves the national AI agenda, the AI Factory's assignment to the FCT, the sectoral data spaces —
have all their claims resting on a page that returns 2,346 bytes and **22 characters** of visible
text. Rejecting them would have said the newsroom does not want them. It wants them more than
anything else in the delivery. They stay undecided, and issue 006 is the one thing that unblocks all
three: find one PDF.

**Putting `build/entregas.py` into the pipeline found that it had never been in it.** The editor's
decisions live in `redacao/revisoes/`, and nothing in `build/tudo.py` read them — an approval
written by a human reached no page, and gate 13 went on believing nothing had been approved. It is
in the pipeline now, anchored to the register's newest capture rather than to the clock, because
`--date` defaults to today and a re-check run the day after a freeze looks in a directory that does
not exist.

**And that is how the worst defect in this release was found.** The same frozen PDF, with the same
SHA-256 re-verified by gate 1, read **30,808 characters on 14 September and 0 on 15 September**. The
bytes had not changed; the machine had. `build/pdf.py` does AES by importing pycryptodome, the
package was not installed here, `_aes_cbc` returned `None`, and the reader concluded the diploma was
**«provavelmente um PDF digitalizado»** — a false statement about somebody else's document, made
with the build green and both claims resting on it silently turned to `fonte_inacessivel`. The
AES-256 path already names the missing package; the AESV2 path says nothing. `requirements.txt` now
names both dependencies with the reason for each and CI installs them before the gates, but the fix
itself is behind the deny list and is the editor's: issue 005. **Evidence that depends on which
machine reads it is not evidence.**

**Two duplicate renderers died.** Publishing exercised a path that predated the dated folders:
every published article was rendered a second time at `artigos/<slug>.html` from `conteudo/<slug>.md`,
and the front-page lead linked to that dead address. Nothing had ever caught it because nothing had
ever been published. Content exists once.

**The archive is the complete list, and the front page is a choice.** `/artigos/` now groups
everything published by the **day it was published** — a different date from the one in the path,
which is the day the bytes were frozen, and both are shown. The front page carries a published block
above «em preparação», because an article the editor published into none of the three bespoke slots
used to exist and be reachable from nowhere.
